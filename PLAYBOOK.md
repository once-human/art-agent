# The playbook

Why the method works: the art is **drawn by code that behaves like a hand**, and an agent **looks at what it made, the way an artist steps back from the easel**, again and again. Nothing is generated by an image model, so nothing has that look. Every mark is a decision you can change.

## 0. Talk before you draw

- Brainstorm in chat first. Offer 2-4 genuinely different directions, each in a few lines: the idea, what is in the picture, the medium, why it suits the person it is for. Then wait for a pick.
- Find out **who it is for** before anything else. The owner once threw out four ideas because they were drawn for the wrong audience (see LESSONS.md). Ask yourself: who will wear or hang this, where do they come from, what do they love about the subject?
- Check the facts of the place and moment (what really stands there, what time things close, what the weather is). True details make art feel lived-in. A wrong one breaks it.
- Never reach for clip-art landmarks or folk motifs as a shortcut. Pick the feeling, then the things that carry it.

## 1. Write the brief (the most important step)

Use [prompts/brief-template.md](prompts/brief-template.md). A good brief is long, often 1-2 pages. The brief behind the best piece was about 1,500 words. It has:

1. **Who it is for**, and what was already rejected and why, in the owner's own words.
2. **The piece**: title, the feeling, the moment (time, weather, light).
3. **What can be in it**: a generous list of possible contents (people with character, vehicles, architecture, weather, small life), each with true details. Say plainly that it is a menu to edit hard, not a checklist.
4. **Art direction**: the medium options to choose between, with the rule "pick ONE coherent language and commit". The composition: focal point, depth, big shapes first, edges that dissolve. The palette and its limit.
5. **The anti-AI rules** from RULES.md, pasted in full as hard rules.
6. **Output limits** from OUTPUTS.md: canvas size, binary alpha, minimum feature, margins, ink layers.
7. **Tech**: Python + Pillow + numpy, a seeded `gen.py`, draft resolution for most rounds, render time limit, no per-pixel Python loops.
8. **Outputs**: the art, a preview on the final background, and `manifest.json` (concept, medium, palette, calm zones for type, handwritten annotations, focal point).
9. **References to LOOK at** with the Read tool: images of past work as the quality bar ("not the style to copy"), and generators to lift primitives from (this repo's `toolkit/`).

## 2. Run the loop

Use [workflow/paint.js](workflow/paint.js) (Claude Code Workflow tool). Its shape is:

| step | agent | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| Paint | artist | writes PLAN.md, builds the piece in passes, **at least 6** render-look-fix rounds |
| Critique 1 | fresh critic | never saw it being made; looks at the preview, 1:1 crops and a thumbnail; measures the print rules; returns ranked, located fixes |
| Repaint | artist | acts on every point it agrees with, writes down why for the rest; big structural changes welcome; **at least 5** rounds |
| Critique 2 | fresh critic | same as before, on the new state |
| Polish | artist | acts on it, then the up-close details, print checks, full-res final; **at least 4** rounds |

The single-agent version made the first pieces (3 rounds each) and they were already loved. The critics and the extra rounds are there to push further. Never cut the rounds to save time.

### What the artist does in each round

1. Render a draft (`R=1`, 1200x1600 or similar).
2. **Look** at the whole preview with the Read tool, and at 1:1 crops of the full-res art (`toolkit/check.py crops`): the focal point, the people, one textured area.
3. Append a harsh critique to CRITIQUE.md: would a working illustrator be proud of this? What reads as computer-made, stiff, cloned, empty, muddy, flat, generic or AI-ish? Is the focal point clear at thumbnail size? Do the people have character?
4. Fix the worst 3-5 things. Render again. Keep `round-N-preview.png` so the history is visible.
5. Never stop at "good enough". Stop when nothing real is left to fix.

### Build in passes, like an illustrator

1. The composition and big shapes (perspective, horizon, masses), drawn loosely.
2. The light: where it falls, what it touches (on dark grounds, darkness is free and every mark is light).
3. The main areas of texture (water, road, sky, crowd, field).
4. Architecture and setting.
5. The hero objects (vehicles, a stage).
6. People: each one a person with a gesture, clothes, hair and a reason to be there.
7. Small life: rain, birds, signs, embers, a sleeping dog.
8. Print physics and clean-up (OUTPUTS.md), then the type zones.

## 3. Check it yourself before showing anyone

- Look at the final preview and at least three 1:1 crops yourself. Don't take the artist's report as proof.
- Run `python3 toolkit/check.py verify art.png` for binary alpha, minimum feature and margin.
- Then show the owner the preview with a few honest lines on what is strongest and what is still weakest.

## 4. Lettering is separate

Titles, dates and notes are set afterwards as real vector lettering (Figma or a font pipeline), over the calm zones the artist reported. The artist draws no text unless the brief says so. The handwritten notes in the manifest are lowercase, specific and true.

## Optional: the light loop (only when the owner asks for it)

Use this only when the owner explicitly asks for a quicker pass with fewer critics. Otherwise run the full loop above.

- A short brief (about half a page) instead of the long one.
- About three render, look, fix rounds by the artist.
- One fresh critic pass.
- One rework, then the final.

He asked for this once, for a batch of 32 shirt fronts: "one round pass each... make it quicker... Don't make it non-human art." The anti-AI rules and the print checks stay exactly the same.
