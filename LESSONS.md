# Lessons from the owner

What he loved, what he threw out, in his words where possible. Read before brainstorming.

## Loved
- The line-ink crowd seen from behind and the printmaker map of the race course (examples/images). Why: "it looks much more human art... not like AI generated art". The hand, the character of the people, the true details of the place, the restraint (few inks, one accent).
- The process itself: a long, specific brief + an agent that renders, looks, critiques harshly and redraws, round after round.

## Threw out
- **A first version that looked like "an A4 sheet slapped on a tee"**: flat, typographic, template-like. He wants graphics-heavy, artistic.
- **A heat-camera crowd and a metro-line diagram**: "AI-ish".
- **Textures/objects that "look AI generated"**: anything too smooth, too perfect, too symmetrical, too evenly scattered.
- **Ideas for the wrong audience**: for the Bengaluru shirt he rejected rangoli, jasmine strings and bus destination boards because the buyers are not locals: "They are more from outside, but then they love Bangalore... international... a lot more sophisticated... They won't even know what the fuck is that." The right ideas were the things that crowd actually loves: city lights, the weather, the rain, night streets, autos and cabs, pubs and rooftops.
- **Generic merch ideas** (tracklists, slogans, icons) and **brushstroke lettering on its own**: "think more artistic".

## Working with him
- Brainstorm in chat first, wait for a pick, then build.
- Copy the method, not the style. The style must suit the piece: "that was made specifically for the map... This is not a map."
- When he gives a direction, follow its exact wording. Don't add things he didn't ask for, and don't drop things he did.
- He checks the results himself; show the final preview and be honest about weak spots.
- Real places, real facts: verify, don't invent.
