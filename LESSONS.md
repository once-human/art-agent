# Lessons from the owner

What he loved, what he threw out, in his words where possible. Read before brainstorming.

## Loved
- The line-ink crowd seen from behind and the printmaker map of the race course (examples/images). Why: "it looks much more human art... not like AI generated art". The hand, the character of the people, the true details of the place, the restraint (few inks, one accent).
- The process itself: a long, specific brief + an agent that renders, looks, critiques harshly and redraws, round after round.

## Threw out
- **A first version that looked like "an A4 sheet slapped on a tee"**: flat, typographic, template-like. He wants graphics-heavy, artistic.
- **A heat-camera crowd and a metro-line diagram**: "AI-ish".
- **Textures/objects that "look AI generated"**: anything too smooth, too perfect, too symmetrical, too evenly scattered.
- **Ideas for the wrong audience**: for the Bengaluru shirt he rejected rangoli, jasmine strings and bus destination boards because the buyers are not locals: "They are more from outside, but then they love Bangalore... international... a lot more sophisticated... They won't even know what the fuck is that." The right ideas were the things that crowd actually loves: city lights, the weather, the rain, night streets, autos and cabs, pubs and rooftops.
- **Noise**: a night street built from thousands of thin strokes, specks and fragments. "It looks noisy... no details at all", "I do not even understand what the white thing is" (a canopy drawn as speckle), "why is your artist creating things from like the smallest of strokes or like the smallest of dots". Build from big confident shapes; every shape must read as what it is from arm's length; fine detail only at the focal point.
- **Colour, clarified**: after "use real colors from the real world" he explained he meant only the iconic objects (the Bengaluru auto's green and yellow): "you don't have to use real skin colors... Don't use real colors everywhere. You are free to use whatever you want." Iconic objects keep their true colours; everything else gets a bold, designed, harmonious palette (skin and clothes can take the stage-light colours).
- **Busy scene vs. T-shirt graphic**: a painted night scene with everything in it (stage, crowd, viaduct, autos, cabs) built from flat polygons read as crude cut paper. He wants "much more illustrative... very nice t-shirt graphics... it knows where it is going": one hero moment, clear hierarchy, a designed vignette, people properly drawn with anatomy and gesture.
- **Right place, wrong subject**: a lovely city street for a concert shirt: "this is a nice Bangalore shirt, but then it's not Fred again... nothing concert". The subject has to be the reason the piece exists (the show), with the place as its setting.
- He did like the wet-road reflections in that piece: lights doubled as long broken reflections.
- **Cartoonish vs artistic**: making it "more realistic" with flat polygon people and few big objects read as cartoonish: "not artistic and contemporary". The Mumbai map is the bar: rich, many real things to discover, each hand-drawn and readable, one strong structure, one expressive gesture. Richness is many identifiable objects, not many tiny marks.
- **Don't let one icon take over**: the Bengaluru auto is iconic but "not the main focus point, because then we'll need to rename the t-shirt to auto". The city is the hero; icons are details in it.
- **Realism where it is checkable**: a concert stage should be built like a real open-ground show (scaffold roof, line arrays, LED screens, delay towers, sound-desk tent, barricades), and a metro must read as a metro.
- **Generic merch ideas** (tracklists, slogans, icons) and **brushstroke lettering on its own**: "think more artistic".

## Working with him
- Brainstorm in chat first, wait for a pick, then build.
- Copy the method, not the style. The style must suit the piece: "that was made specifically for the map... This is not a map."
- When he gives a direction, follow its exact wording. Don't add things he didn't ask for, and don't drop things he did.
- He checks the results himself; show the final preview and be honest about weak spots.
- Real places, real facts: verify, don't invent.
